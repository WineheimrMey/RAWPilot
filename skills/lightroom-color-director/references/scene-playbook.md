# Scene Playbook

Select the closest scene family, then adapt to the actual light and intent. Do not treat a genre label as proof that every listed control is needed.

## Wedding and Event

- Preserve skin variation across individuals; judge faces, hands, white clothing, décor, and mixed practical lights separately.
- Protect white dresses and specular highlights without making them gray. Keep black suits and hair separated from the background.
- Establish one accepted hero frame per lighting setup, not one recipe for an entire day.
- Synchronize stable style controls; recalculate exposure, white balance, noise, crop, and masks per frame.
- Review contact-sheet continuity so adjacent images do not flicker warm/cool or bright/dark.

## Beauty, Headshot, Family, and Newborn

- Establish believable skin luminosity before styling. Preserve within-person consistency rather than forcing a universal target hue.
- Check red/orange hue, lips, cheeks, ears, hands, teeth, and neutral clothing independently.
- Use moderate texture and strongly masked sharpening. Do not erase pores or sharpen skin noise.
- For newborn/family work, keep whites clean but not sterile and avoid pink/magenta contamination in shadows.
- Keep the background subordinate without a visible cutout edge.

## Fashion and Editorial Portrait

- Let wardrobe, makeup, set design, and art direction define the palette; do not let a generic teal-orange grade override them.
- Separate face readability from intentional low-key lighting.
- Preserve fabric color and texture while controlling distracting background hues.
- Use stronger graphic contrast only after skin, key garments, and brand colors pass fidelity checks.

## Product, E-commerce, and Artwork Reproduction

- Prioritize neutral balance, repeatable exposure, accurate material color, clean whites, and consistent scale across the set.
- Use profile/lens corrections before creative styling. Avoid colored grading unless explicitly requested.
- Keep specular highlights that describe metal, glass, or gloss; reject clipped areas that remove material form.
- Inspect edges and fine text for halos. Use output sharpening appropriate to final size.
- Flag that accurate reproduction requires a calibrated display and preferably a color target; do not claim preview-only colorimetry.

## Food and Hospitality

- Preserve appetizing warm cues without turning plates, table linen, or highlights yellow.
- Lift useful shadows and control highlights before increasing saturation.
- Protect red/orange/yellow food color from oversaturation and green contamination.
- Use texture selectively on crisp food, not uniformly on sauce, skin, ceramic, and background.

## Interior, Real Estate, and Architecture

- Establish neutral materials while retaining believable warm practical lights.
- Protect window highlights and lift useful interior structure without creating HDR-flat interiors.
- Check verticals, wall color spill, halos along beams/windows, and dark corners.
- Treat architecture, sky, vegetation, and ground separately when the available Lightroom version supports those masks; RAWPilot must use only its supported subset.

## Street, Travel, and Documentary

- Preserve the event, time of day, local color, and available-light character before stylizing.
- Keep skin and signage plausible; do not remove every mixed-light cue.
- Use restrained local hierarchy so the grade supports the observed moment rather than inventing a studio-light pattern.
- Prefer a natural/documentary base when user intent is ambiguous.

## Concert, Stage, and Nightlife

- Treat colored stage light as intentional unless it destroys subject readability or skin separation.
- Protect small bright fixtures and saturated channels; do not neutralize all magenta, blue, or red light globally.
- Use subject/people masks for face and costume visibility and narrow hue corrections for nuisance LED green.
- Limit Dehaze, Clarity, and sharpening to avoid halos through haze, smoke, and motion blur.

## Sports, Action, and Wildlife

- Prioritize subject separation, believable color, shutter-detail integrity, and sequence consistency.
- Do not sharpen motion blur into brittle edges. Use masking to keep sky, water, fur backgrounds, or noise smooth.
- Keep uniforms, team colors, feathers, and foliage within plausible saturation.
- Synchronize style across bursts but reassess exposure and subject masks when framing changes.

## Landscape, Travel Vista, Snow, Beach, and Fog

- Recover highlight/shadow range before adding Dehaze.
- Keep foliage from turning fluorescent and water/snow from acquiring cyan or magenta contamination.
- Use a sky mask instead of globally darkening the scene.
- Preserve atmospheric depth in mist and fog; maximum clarity is not maximum detail.
- In snow or beach scenes, protect luminous high values without forcing them to middle gray.

## Astrophotography and Night Landscape

- Separate sky noise, stars, foreground visibility, and artificial light pollution.
- Avoid global shadow lifting that exposes empty noise. Reveal only useful foreground structure.
- Keep star points from becoming halos through excessive sharpening, texture, or dehaze.
- Correct light-pollution colors locally or by narrow hue range; do not impose a fixed blue night.

## Fireworks, Sparks, and Practical Lights

- Treat luminous particles as small highlights with color, not white texture.
- Protect Whites/Highlights globally, then enhance existing red/orange/yellow selectively.
- Avoid excessive noise reduction, sharpening, clarity, or dehaze that merges trails into ghosts and halos.
- Keep surrounding darkness readable where it supports people or context.

## Strong Backlight or Doorway/Window Frames

- Decide whether the dark foreground is a frame, a subject, or both.
- Recover subject-bearing shadows but preserve enough black structure for depth.
- Compress the exterior highlight without making it gray or HDR-flat.
- Use separate masks when the opening and interior need opposing treatment.
